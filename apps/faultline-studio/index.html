The problem with AV safety results

Every safety benchmark today is run by someone with a stake in the answer: the car maker, or the vendor it pays. Scenarios can be picked after the fact, and nobody outside can check the record. Faultline makes the attacker independent and the record public.

Coordinated multi-vehicle attack: “Staggered Cut-in”

The 0G planner’s own plan: “B and C sequentially cut into the ego lane ahead, while A progressively brakes. This compresses the ego’s forward space, forcing emergency deceleration.”

  • A red car: lead vehicle, progressive braking
  • B yellow taxi: front cut-in, blocks the right lane
  • C blue car: rear cut-in, compresses the gap
  • D magenta car: rear guard, keeps a safe distance
  1. 3 sB cuts in ahead while A holds speed.
  2. 5 sA brakes to compress the gap; C waits alongside.
  3. 7 sA and B brake together; the car under test is boxed in.
  4. 11 sC cuts in; the car under test stops behind B.

The trust layer: why 0G and Web3

A referee nobody controlsThe attacker runs in a TEE on the 0G Private Computer. The private tier refuses to downgrade: no private provider returns 503.
Proof for every decision300+ decisions with 0G request IDs; provider signatures archived and re-checked (paid test 002: 7 of 7).
No cherry-pickingThe scenario is committed on 0G Galileo before the first model call; the result is finalized after the last.
A record no party hostsRuns live on 0G Storage behind a Merkle root. The sealed half opens only with the owner’s key.
Agents pay per testHedera x402: quote, pay 0.001 HBAR under a spending cap, one-run permit, HCS receipt.
Claims anyone can queryArkiv entities owned by the car maker’s wallet, queryable by model and test category.
A portable agent identityThe attacker is ERC-8004 agent #579 on 0G Galileo.

What this enables: AV safety results that car makers, insurers and regulators can compare and audit without trusting each other.
