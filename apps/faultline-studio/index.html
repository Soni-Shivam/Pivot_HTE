Autonomous vehicle red-teaming

Your autonomous car passed the test. Who tested the tester?

Faultline uses AI-controlled traffic adversaries to challenge autonomous vehicles in CARLA, then publishes independently checkable evidence of every decision and outcome.

Explore real attack Verify evidence

Faultline doesn’t ask you to trust the AI testing your car. It gives you the experiment, the evidence, and the means to challenge both.

The trust problem

Every safety benchmark today is run by someone with a stake in the answer: the car maker, or the vendor it pays. Scenarios can be picked after the fact, and nobody outside can check the record. Faultline makes the attacker independent and the record public.

One attack, four AI-controlled cars

Before the first tick, the 0G strategist wrote a plan named “Staggered Cut-in”: “B and C sequentially cut into the ego lane ahead, while A progressively brakes. This compresses the ego’s forward space, forcing emergency deceleration.”

    1. 3 sB cuts in ahead while A holds speed.
    2. 5 sA brakes to compress the gap; C waits alongside.
    3. 7 sA and B brake together; the car under test is boxed in.
    4. 11 sC cuts in; the car under test stops behind B.

          Test your own AV stack
          Faultline owns the world, the attacker and the clock. Your stack owns the car’s decisions. One small interface connects them.
          
      
          
        
          Five steps
          
        
            
          
          1. Pick an adapter. Python in-process, an existing CARLA Leaderboard agent (no changes), or ROS 2 (Autoware and similar).
            2. 
          
          2. Write stack.yaml. Name, adapter, entry point or sensors, latency budget, and the files whose digest identifies your stack.
            3. 
          
          3. Run the checker. python -m faultline.stack check stack.yaml checks sensors against the live CARLA 0.10 blueprints, controls, latency, determinism and the digest.
            4. 
          
          4. Dry run for free. python -m faultline.stack.arena --site seed:0 --stacks stack.yaml --mode script; add a second stack for an A/B comparison.
            5. 
          
          5. Buy a paid run. The Hedera permit binds the digest from step 3, so the run you pay for is provably your declared stack.
            6.
